## Assumptions and jokes (not claims)

- "Her crush was supercooled", "physicists call that bump a nucleation site; your friend calls it...", "she's not picky, that's just physics": the joke frame. Physics doesn't describe crushes.
- "He clapped when the plane landed" is a common example of an ick online, used as the friend's example, not a study finding.
- "Turning to ice all at once": in a real supercooled bottle only part of the water freezes at first (the released heat warms it to 0 °C, giving slush); the bottle icing over completely is a cartoon simplification.
- "Single women" in the script: the study sampled single adults; the 75% is the share of its women.
- The freezer display counting down to -40 °C is illustrative: a home freezer runs at about -18 °C, and tap water in a bottle usually freezes well above -40 °C because of impurities.
- Sources pill on the last shot: "Data: Collisson, Saunders & Yin 2025; AMS Glossary of Meteorology".
