# The Yawn Test

- **id:** 2026-11-06-yawn-test
- **Source idea:** ours. A tag-a-friend joke: catching your friend's yawn is a friendship test. Norscia and Palagi (PLoS ONE, 2011) watched 109 adults yawn in everyday life for a year: yawns spread most from family, then friends, then acquaintances, and least from strangers, and family and friends caught them faster.
- **Why people care:** everybody yawns back at someone, and "if you yawned watching this, tag who you thought of" is a send-to-one-person line. Viewers will yawn while watching, which is the joke.
- **Format:** toon kit (toon.py), calm Chatterbox read, yellow outlined captions.
- **Rotation:** the episode before it is The Friend Who Can't Pick A Restaurant (pastel toy kit, sidewalk, grocery store). The toon episode before that, The NASA Nap, was a library, a cockpit and a movie screen. This one: a movie-night living room with two friends on a beanbag pair, then a city bus with a row of riders (family, friend, coworker, stranger) for the ranking, and a stopwatch board. No library, no cockpit. Opening: your friend mid-yawn, mouth wide open, with you already starting to yawn next to them, on frame one.
- **Odd physical detail:** each yawn lets out a little puff cloud that drifts to the next person and makes them yawn; the stranger's puff takes ages to land.
- **Files:** `pipeline/render_yawn.py`, `pipeline/yawn_scene.py`.

## Script: see `SCRIPT` in `pipeline/render_yawn.py`

1. You just yawned because your friend yawned. That's a friendship test.
2. Researchers in Italy watched 109 adults yawn in real life for a whole year.
3. Yawns spread the most between family. Then friends.
4. Then people you barely know. Strangers came last.
5. Most family yawned back within a minute. About half of friends did too.
6. Strangers mostly took two or three minutes.
7. So if your friend yawns and you don't, think about that.
8. Tag the friend who just made you yawn.

The last line runs back into the first.

## Assumptions and jokes

- "Most family" stands for "about two-thirds of relatives" (the press quote). "Strangers mostly took two or three minutes" is the lead author's description, not a table value.
- "Friendship test" and "think about that" are jokes. The study shows a link with social closeness; not yawning back does not prove anything about one friendship, and the on-screen note says "Norscia & Palagi, PLoS ONE, 2011".
- The friends, the bus and the riders are fiction. No real people on screen.
