# Napoleon wakes up in 2026

- **id:** 2026-10-28-napoleon-2026
- **Source idea:** Austin's DM, Oct 27 (https://www.instagram.com/p/De-awMiNkHA/, @whatif.bros, a voxel "what if a dictator woke up in 2026" reel): "make your own take on this". Our own subject (Napoleon) and every line is ours. Satire of a historical figure: the joke is on him (his height, his hat, Waterloo), never on anyone he ruled or fought.
- **Format:** 3D voxel deadpan short in a new set (a museum gallery with a hat case and a height chart), about 40 s.

## Script (render_tap markup)

1. *Napoleon* wakes up in *2026.*
2. Last thing he remembers: *Saint Helena,* *1821.*
3. He's in a *museum,* next to one of his own *hats.*
4. One of them sold in *2023* for *1.9 million euros.* He owned about *a hundred and twenty.*
5. He looks himself up. The internet says he was *short.*
6. He was about *one sixty-nine.* *Average* for a Frenchman back then.
7. British cartoonists drew him *tiny,* and it *stuck.* There's a *complex* named after him.
8. His *franc* got replaced by the *euro* in *2002.*
9. He checks the weather in *Moscow.* He closes the app.
10. He asks about *Waterloo.* It's an *ABBA* song now. It won *Eurovision.*
11. He has now lost Waterloo *twice.*

## Assumptions, written out

- The premise (he wakes up in 2026) is Austin's requested format, said plainly as a what-if, not a claim.
- "He's in a museum next to one of his own hats": the gallery and case are made up. The hat in the case is not claimed to be the one sold in 2023; the script says "one of them sold".
- "One sixty-nine" means 1.69 m; the on-screen card spells it out (about 1.69 m, 5 ft 6 or 7). Sources give 5 ft 6 to 5 ft 7.
- The Moscow beat is a nod to his 1812 retreat; the weather card says only "winter is coming", no invented temperature.
- "Lost Waterloo twice" is the joke: the 1815 battle, and now the song.
- I left out the often-quoted line that Gillray "did more than all the armies of Europe" to bring him down: no primary source found.
