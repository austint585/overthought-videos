## Script (render_tap markup)

1. Right now, tiny animals are living on your *face.*
2. They're called *face mites.* More than *90 percent* of people have them.
3. Each one is under a *third of a millimeter* long, living in your *pores,* sucking up your skin *grease.*
4. And at *night,* while you're asleep, they crawl *out.*
5. They walk across your face, find each other, and *mate* on your *skin.*
6. People even said they had no *anus,* and held it in their whole *lives.*
7. A 2022 study looked. They *do.*
8. So yeah. Right now, tiny animals are living on your *face.*

## Assumptions, written out

- "People even said" is deliberate: Perotti says the anus was described decades earlier, so the no-anus story was a myth rather than the settled science. The 2022 team confirmed it; they were not the first to see it.
- 90 percent is one researcher's figure, also repeated by Popular Science.
- Mites are drawn larger than life in relation to pores and the follicle, and the follicle cut is schematic; on-screen notes say so. Head-down posture is an illustration and is not claimed in the script.
- The mites shown meeting only touch heads.
