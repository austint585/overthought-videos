## Facts and sources

| Claim in the script | Source |

| The bystander effect: people are less likely to help when they believe others are also present | Darley and Latane, "Bystander intervention in emergencies: diffusion of responsibility", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 8(4):377-383 (1968). Textbook summary: https://opentextbc.ca/researchmethods/part/experimental-research/ |
| Students over an intercom heard what sounded like another student having a seizure (it was a recording) | Same study; summaries above and https://savemyexams.com/dp/psychology/ib/17/hl/revision-notes/human-relationships/social-responsibility-bystanderism-and-prosocial-behaviour/two-key-studies-of-bystanderism |

## Assumptions and jokes (not claims)

- The group chat is our framing: the study was about an emergency, not texts. The script says only what the study found and lets the viewer make the link.
- "Fourteen people saw it" and "Seen by 14" are the joke setup, not data.
- "Ask one person, by name" is advice that follows from diffusion of responsibility, not a number from this study.
- The bar heights match 85% and 31%; the running phone and the HELP door are illustration.
- Sources pill on the last shot: "Data: Darley & Latane 1968, J. of Personality and Social Psychology".
