# The NASA Nap

- **id:** 2026-11-06-nasa-nap
- **Source idea:** ours. A tag-a-friend joke: everyone has the friend whose "quick nap" swallows the whole afternoon. NASA really did test naps, on airline pilots crossing the Pacific overnight: about 26 minutes of sleep made them 34% better on performance tests and 54% more alert than pilots who didn't nap. Your friend's nap is the length of Titanic.
- **Why people care:** the twenty-minute nap that turns into three hours is universal, and "the length of Titanic" is a send-to-one-person line.
- **Format:** toon kit (toon.py), calm Chatterbox read, yellow outlined captions.

No couch, no bed. Opening: the friend snoring face down on an open textbook, a phone alarm on "20 MIN" flashing beside them, on frame one.

## Script: see `SCRIPT` in `pipeline/render_nap.py`

1. Your friend's twenty minute nap just hit hour three.
2. NASA actually tested naps, on pilots flying overnight across the Pacific.
3. Pilots got a 40 minute window to rest in their seat. They slept about 26 minutes.
4. After that nap, they did 34 percent better on tests than pilots who didn't nap.
5. And their alertness was up 54 percent.
6. Twenty-six minutes. That's the NASA nap.
7. Your friend's nap is the length of Titanic.
8. Tag the friend whose twenty minute nap just hit hour three.

The last line runs back into the first.

## Assumptions and jokes

- "Overnight" means the long-haul night flights in the study; the study was flights across the Pacific, which the script says.
- "Tests" stands for the reaction-time vigilance tests the crews took; "alertness" is the EEG-based measure. The signs read "+34% PERFORMANCE" and "+54% ALERTNESS", and the note reads "NASA (Rosekind et al.), 1994".
- The friend's three-hour nap, the library and the Titanic comparison are jokes. The ship on the movie screen is a generic toy boat, not the film's imagery, and no title art is used.
- No real people on screen.
