## Script (render_tap markup)

1. Right now, your stomach is trying to *digest* you.
2. It's full of *acid,* around pH *two,* and an enzyme called *pepsin* that breaks down meat.
3. And your stomach wall is *meat.*
4. The only thing saving you is a layer of *mucus,* packed with *bicarbonate* that kills the acid before it touches you.
5. And the cells under it wear out so fast, your stomach replaces its lining every *three to five days.*
6. When that slime fails, from *bacteria* or too many *painkillers,* the acid eats right into you.
7. That's called an *ulcer.*
8. So yeah. Right now, your stomach is trying to *digest* you.

## Assumptions, written out

- "Trying to digest you" is a dramatization: acid and pepsin would attack the lining if the barrier weren't there; the stomach has no intent.
- "Your stomach wall is meat": the wall is muscle and protein, the same kind of tissue pepsin breaks down in food.
- "Kills the acid" is the plain version of "neutralizes" (bicarbonate raises the pH at the cell surface).
- "Wear out so fast": surface cells are shed and replaced; sources range from 1 to 7 days, and we say the common 3 to 5.
- "Bacteria" means H. pylori; "too many painkillers" is NSAIDs. Neither always causes an ulcer.
- Pictures are illustrations: the acid is colored yellow-green so it reads (real gastric juice is nearly clear), pepsin is a blob, cell sizes and the mucus layer are not to scale, and the cell replacement is a time-lapse.
