# Style notes from Austin's reference reels

Austin DMs other creators' AI and 3D reels, sometimes pointing at one thing he likes. Each line: the date, the reel link, and the aspect in our own words. The production run reads this before making episodes and applies it.

- 2026-10-26: https://www.instagram.com/p/De7GWzZgy2d/ and https://www.instagram.com/p/De9tphNA-Xj/ (@larpforlarp). Deadpan straight-faced delivery of absurd slang, a title card hook, word-by-word captions, about 30 to 36 s, one fake-academic closing line. The humor is the slang density: keep the buzzwords.
- 2026-10-26: @nichelarp65 (account, not a DM). "What if you got 2x more [habit] every second?" with "you" as the subject, a counter panel top-left, labels on the scene, 3 to 5 posts a day. Hits are relatable vices and identity; a "tag your friend who..." caption.
- 2026-10-27: Austin, with the @_nofoodwasted_ steak clip: the TikTok "children of khan" type of videos, PS2-era animation style, framed like "how that one dude in the back of class..." with a lot of wit. Look up that style before the next classroom or everyday-character episode.
- 2026-10-27: https://www.instagram.com/p/De_42nKxoyZ/ (@how_it_secretly_works). What Austin likes: an everyday object explained in clean 3D, opened with a hook that makes a familiar button or habit feel like it secretly matters to you, then the mechanism. Our everyday explainers should open with a hook that strong (a real, sourced consequence), not a plain "how does X work".
- 2026-10-27: https://www.instagram.com/p/De-f9_QJCjm/ (@petersindex). Austin asked what makes this style. Soft claymation look: every object reads as handmade plasticine with fingerprint texture and rounded edges, felt or wool for smoke and clouds, a tiny tilt-shift world with shallow depth of field, warm lamp light against a blue night, cuts every 1 to 2 s, and one short phrase at a time in a cream rounded label with a bold rounded font and one word in blue. Try a clay material pass (subsurface, soft bump noise, low gloss) and real depth of field on the voxel scenes.
- 2026-10-28: https://www.instagram.com/p/De-f9_QJCjm/ (@petersindex), again. Austin wants to move off the blocky voxel look and asked whether we can do this clay style as easily. Materials, depth of field and lamp-against-night lighting are straightforward in Blender; the hard part is soft, rounded, expressive figures instead of voxel blocks. Plan: a short clay-look test on an existing set before switching the production runs.
- 2026-10-28: https://www.instagram.com/p/De_42nKxoyZ/ (@how_it_secretly_works), again, for the voice: Austin wants a narrator that feels more personable than ours (warm, conversational, talking straight to "you", about 170 words a minute). Use a voice we have rights to that feels like it; never clone that creator's voice from the reel.
- 2026-10-28: Austin's tone rule: any reel that is straight educational (not satire or a joke) gets a narrator who yells and screams the whole way through. The joke is calm, useful content delivered by a guy screaming for no reason. Satire keeps the deadpan voice. Kokoro cannot shout, so this waits for a voice engine that can; until then, make satire episodes rather than straight educational ones.
